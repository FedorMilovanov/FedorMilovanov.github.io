Visual direction and review

Different products need different visual languages

A research library, a poetry platform, and a commercial storefront should not share one generic template. I work to identify each product's character, define a direction, review composition, and achieve visual consistency without unnecessary spectacle.

My role: setting visual requirements, selecting a direction, reviewing typography, composition and mobile behavior, giving feedback, and accepting revisions.

Authorship boundary: some images, code, layouts and design alternatives were produced with AI and other tools. These examples do not establish hands-on Figma proficiency, formal UX research or improved conversion rates.

Gospod Bog — Sila Moya · live website · 5 Nov 2026

01 / Research library

Quiet presentation that serves the text

Paper-like textures, restrained gold, original languages and generous serif typography give the site the character of a study library rather than an entertainment portal.

Open design consideration: long-form reading comfort, navigation and visual consistency of interactive elements on mobile.

The Legendary Poet · live website · 5 Nov 2026

02 / Literary platform

A digital museum for poetry

A dark canvas, cool blue glow and arched portraits create an identity distinct from the academic library. The visual treatment is deliberately more dramatic.

Open trade-off: the oversized desktop heading competes with content above the fold; mobile captions and navigation require careful legibility checks.

Milovi Cake · live website · 5 Nov 2026

03 / Commercial storefront

Warm color and authentic product imagery

The design prioritizes the cake itself, its texture and presentation, supported by restrained cocoa-and-gold tones instead of generic cards or ornamental effects.

Open trade-off: colorful messenger and rating badges are useful but sometimes disrupt the otherwise controlled palette.

Editorial design rather than a catalog
